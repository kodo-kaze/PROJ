# Algorithm Proposal: Multi-Stage Spatial-Temporal Crowd Pipeline

## 1. Problem Statement
Managing high-density pedestrian environments requires predicting both the macroscopic flow of the crowd and the microscopic trajectories of individuals. Traditional data-driven models frequently fail during unseen emergency evacuations (out-of-distribution scenarios). Furthermore, at extreme densities, standard 2D collision-avoidance models break down because physical contact forces supersede human routing choices, leading to crowd collapses. Finally, attempting to predict complex crowd interactions using deep Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) introduces a computational bottleneck (over-smoothing and under-reaching). 

This algorithm solves these issues by proposing a 3-stage hybrid pipeline: it maps continuous density, applies a biomechanical physics threshold to detect critical crush conditions[cite: 1], and uses a highly efficient Virtual Graph Trajectory Expert (ViTE) to route pedestrians safely *before* physical forces take over.

## 2. Input and Output
* **Inputs:**
  * `VideoStream S`: Continuous CCTV camera feed bounded by a specific `RegionOfInterest (ROI)`.
  * `HistoricalFlow X`: Spatial flow rates (inflow/outflow) captured across the sensor network[cite: 3].
  * `CriticalForceThreshold`: The biomechanical limit where physical contact forces ($F_c$) overwhelm social driving forces ($F_{drv}$)[cite: 1].
* **Outputs:**
  * `HazardLevel`: System status (`SAFE`, `HIGH_THROUGHPUT`, or `CRITICAL_CRUSH`), evaluated using a Weighted Average Error (WAE) metric focused on the 0.9 quantile to capture extreme density spikes[cite: 3].
  * `OptimalPaths Y`: Bivariate Gaussian trajectory coordinates for collision-free routing, generated by the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) router.
