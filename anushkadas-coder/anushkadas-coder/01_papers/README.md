# Research Papers (2025)

This directory contains the raw PDF downloads for the initial literature review phase, focusing strictly on recent algorithmic approaches to the pedestrian crowd problem.

### 1. Evaluating Crowd Flow Forecasting Algorithms for Indoor Pedestrian Spaces: A Benchmark Using a Synthetic Dataset
* **Core Focus:** Data-Driven Flow Forecasting & Synthetic Benchmarking
* **Summary:** This paper addresses the critical shortage of high-quality, large-scale empirical data for indoor crowd tracking. It proposes a benchmark evaluation pipeline that generates synthetic realistic scenarios via simulation. By training data-driven predictive models on this synthetic dataset, the research provides a robust framework to improve indoor architectural planning and evacuation routing without relying on hard-to-acquire real-world data.

### 2. Exploring Dense Crowd Dynamics: State of the Art and Emerging Paradigms
* **Core Focus:** 3D Multiscale Modeling & Physical Interaction
* **Summary:** Moving beyond traditional 2D collision avoidance, this research advocates for a 3D multiscale modeling approach in extreme-density scenarios. It focuses on mapping biomechanical mechanisms and physical force transmissions (like wedging and balance recovery). This is crucial for predicting and mitigating dangerous collective behaviors, such as spontaneous oscillatory motion or crowd collapses, before they escalate.

### 3. Vite: Virtual Graph Trajectory Expert Router for Pedestrian Trajectory Prediction
* **Core Focus:** Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) & Trajectory Prediction
* **Summary:** This paper introduces a virtual graph trajectory expert router designed to map and predict the complex, non-linear paths of individuals within a dense crowd. By effectively modeling the social and physical interactions in shared spaces, this architecture overcomes the inherent unpredictability of human movement, making it highly applicable for automated surveillance and early warning systems against crowd crush risks.
